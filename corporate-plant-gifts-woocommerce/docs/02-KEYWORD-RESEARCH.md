# Keyword research and mapping

## How this research was done

Live SERP research against the corporate plant gifting market, reviewing the
pages that currently rank for the head terms and the sub topics they cover.
Sources reviewed included NurseryLive, Ugaoo, Kyari, Greenkin, PlantsGuru,
UnlimitedGreens, GardenGik, IndiaGift, FlowerAura, IGP and Lively Root, plus
Indiamart supplier listings for the bulk and wholesale side of the market.

**A note on the volume column.** No paid keyword API (SemRush, Ahrefs,
DataForSEO) was connected to this session, so the volume figures below are
**estimated bands derived from SERP competition and query patterns, not
measured data**. Treat them as a priority ordering, not as forecasts. Before
you commit budget, run the seed list in `keyword-seed-list.csv` through Google
Keyword Planner or SemRush for your target country and replace the estimates.

What the mapping below *is* reliable for: the topic clusters, the intent
classification, the URL assignments and the internal linking plan. Those come
from what actually ranks, not from a volume tool.

## 1. Intent model for this market

Buyers arrive in one of four states, and each needs a different page type.

| Intent | What they type | Page type that wins |
|---|---|---|
| Commercial investigation | "corporate plant gifts for employees" | Service hub page with proof and process |
| Transactional | "buy succulent gift set online" | Product category page |
| Comparison / spec | "best pot material for succulents" | Category body copy and buying guide |
| Informational | "how often to water a snake plant" | Care guide and journal posts |

The store is structured so that each of these has an owner, and no two pages
compete for the same query.

**Why one page owns all of these:** they resolve to the same answer. Splitting

"corporate plant gifts" also ranks for "plants as corporate gifts" from the
same URL.

## 7. Keyword density guidance

The content in this build deliberately does **not** repeat the focus keyword.
Target frequencies used:

- Focus keyword in the H1 once
- Focus keyword or a close variant in one H2
- Focus keyword in the first 100 words once
- Focus keyword in the meta title and meta description once
- Focus keyword in the URL slug
- Body density around 0.5 to 1.0 percent, reached naturally

Everything else is covered through **entities and synonyms** rather than
repetition. Modern retrieval matches on meaning, and repeating a phrase eleven
times is now a negative signal rather than a positive one.

## 8. Generative engine optimisation notes

The content is structured for AI answer engines as well as classic search.
Specific choices made:

1. **Direct answers first.** Every FAQ answer opens with the answer, then
   explains. Answer engines extract the first sentence.
2. **Specific numbers.** "Water every twelve to fourteen days" rather than
   "water occasionally". Concrete figures are far more quotable.
3. **Stated limitations.** The air purifying category says plainly that a few
   pots will not filter a floor. Content that acknowledges limits is cited more
   readily than content that overclaims, and it is more defensible.
4. **Tables for comparable facts.** Watering intervals, pot sizes, transit
   times and pricing slabs are all in tables, which parse cleanly.
5. **Self contained sections.** Each H2 section can be lifted out and still
   makes sense, which is how retrieval augmented systems chunk a page.
6. **FAQPage, Service, ItemList and Product schema** so machines can read the
   same facts the human reads.
7. **Entity coverage.** Species names, pot materials, branding methods, cities
   and occasions are all named explicitly rather than implied.

## 9. Content gaps to fill next

The build covers the commercial core. These are the highest value additions,
in order:

1. **Comparison posts.** "Succulents vs air plants for corporate gifting",
   "Ceramic vs terracotta pots". Low competition, high intent.
2. **City landing pages.** "Corporate plant gifts in Bangalore" and equivalents
   for your top five delivery cities. Only build these if you have genuinely
   different content per city, otherwise they are thin pages.
3. **Case studies.** "How a 400 person team was gifted in one week." These earn
   links and give sales something to send.
4. **Seasonal hub, refreshed annually.** A Diwali gifting guide updated each
   July rather than a new URL every year.
5. **Care guides per species.** One page per plant, linked from the product.
   These pick up the informational long tail at very low cost.
