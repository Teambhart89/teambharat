Why a plant works as a corporate gift

Most corporate gifts have a short life. The hamper is eaten, the diary goes in a drawer, the branded bottle is used twice. A plant sits on the desk, gets watered, gets noticed by the people walking past, and is still there at the next appraisal cycle with your logo on it.

It only works if the plant survives. That is the whole difference between a good plant gift and a slightly awkward one, and it comes down to choosing species that tolerate an air conditioned office and irregular watering.

When companies order

Festive gifting

Diwali is the largest window by a wide margin, followed by New Year. Both are worth planning early, because printing capacity across the market gets booked out and the comfortable timelines belong to whoever ordered in August.

Onboarding and welcome kits

A plant in a branded pot on a new joiner's desk on day one, ideally with a card that names them. Steady volumes through the year rather than a seasonal spike, which makes them easy to schedule.

Client and partner gifts

Smaller quantities, higher value per unit, usually a larger plant in a good ceramic planter with proper boxing.

Events, conferences and CSR

Attendee giveaways, speaker gifts and plantation drive saplings. Volume is high and the per unit budget is usually tight, which suits succulents and small foliage plants well.

Plants that survive a desk

This is a shorter list than clients expect, and keeping to it is why our gift plants are still alive months later.

The reliable choices

Succulents, snake plant, ZZ plant, lucky bamboo, jade and small aglaonema. Every one of them tolerates low light, forgives a fortnight without water, and stays compact enough to live beside a monitor without becoming a nuisance.

What we steer clients away from

Flowering plants, anything needing bright light, and anything that outgrows a desk within a few months. They photograph beautifully on handover day and turn into a small disappointment three weeks later, which is the opposite of what the gift was for.

For remote and hybrid teams

Plants shipped individually need to survive a courier as well as a desk. Succulents and snake plant travel best. We pack each box to hold the plant and its soil in place, because a plant that arrives with soil across the packaging starts the relationship badly.

Getting your branding onto the pot

Printing

The usual method, and it works well on ceramic, fibre and metal planters. Full colour is possible, though a single colour logo on a plain pot usually looks more considered than a full colour print does.

Engraving

Suits wood and certain ceramic finishes. More understated, more expensive, and the right choice for senior client gifts where restraint reads better than a large logo.

Sleeves, tags and cards

For smaller runs where a printing setup is not worth it, a printed sleeve around the pot or a hanging tag carries the branding at a fraction of the cost. Often the better answer below fifty units.

What we need from your team

Your logo as a vector file, in the colours you actually use rather than approximations. A logo pulled off a website rarely holds up at pot size, and fixing it later costs a day in the schedule.

Approve a physical sample

On every branded order we make one sample with your logo on the actual pot before printing the rest. It is chargeable and adjusted against the final invoice. Skipping it saves a few days and occasionally costs an entire run.

Packing, delivery and the care card

Packing options

Individual gift boxes where each plant is handed over separately, or bulk cartons where your team distributes them internally. Individual boxing costs more per unit and looks considerably better in the recipient's hands.

Delivery

Direct delivery across Delhi NCR to your office or event venue. For teams spread across the country, send a spreadsheet of names and addresses and we ship individually by courier. Individual shipping costs more per unit than a single bulk delivery, so it is worth deciding which route you are taking before the quote is built.

The care card

Every gift includes a small card written for someone who has never kept a plant alive. How often to water, where to put it, and what the first sign of trouble looks like. It costs almost nothing and it noticeably improves how many of your gifts are still alive six months on, which is the only measure of this that matters.

Planning your order

Branded orders take ten to fifteen days from artwork approval, including the sample. Around Diwali that stretches, sometimes considerably. If your gifting date is fixed, start the conversation six to eight weeks ahead and you will get both the stock and the timeline you want.
