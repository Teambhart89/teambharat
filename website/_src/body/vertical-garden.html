How a green wall is actually built

A vertical garden is a system, not a decoration, and understanding the layers explains most of what goes right and wrong with them.

The frame and the gap behind it

A metal or treated batten frame is fixed to the wall so the planting sits a few inches clear of the building surface. That gap is not a detail. It keeps air moving, keeps the wall dry, and is the difference between a green wall and a damp problem.

The waterproof backing

A sealed backing board sits between the planting and the building. Every drop of water that leaves the system is directed downward into a collection channel rather than into the plaster.

The planting layer

Either modular panels holding individual pots, or a felt pocket system where plants root into a fabric medium. Panels are easier to service plant by plant. Felt gives a denser, more continuous surface. We choose between them based on how the wall will be reached for maintenance.

Irrigation and drainage

A drip line runs along the top and feeds down by gravity on a timer, with a channel at the base catching runoff. Many walls run on a recirculating tank, which cuts water use and removes the need for a permanent plumbing connection.

Lighting, where daylight is short

An interior wall with no natural light needs grow lights on a timer. There is no plant selection that avoids this. Where a client would rather not run lighting, we recommend an artificial wall and say so directly.

Live, artificial or moss

These are three different products that happen to look similar in a photograph. Choosing the wrong one is the most common and most expensive mistake in this category.

Live green walls

Right where there is reasonable daylight or a budget for lighting, a water point within reach, and someone accountable for upkeep. A living wall changes through the year, softens sound noticeably in a hard surfaced reception, and reads as genuinely alive in a way nothing else matches.

Artificial green walls

Right for dark corridors, high atrium walls that need scaffolding to reach, exterior positions in harsh western sun, and any site where maintenance will realistically not happen. A good artificial wall kept clean looks far better than a live wall that has been allowed to die, and we would rather install the honest option.

Preserved moss walls

No water, no light, no maintenance beyond keeping them dry and dust free. Excellent in meeting rooms, lift lobbies and interior walls with no services. Keep them out of direct sun and away from humid areas such as an open kitchen, where preserved moss loses its texture over time.

Mixing the three

On large walls we sometimes combine them, using live planting at the reachable lower section and artificial or moss above where access is difficult. Done carefully it reads as one wall and it stays looking right.

Plants that work on a green wall in this climate

Indoor walls

Syngonium, philodendron, money plant, peperomia, ferns and aglaonema. These knit together into a continuous surface rather than sitting as separate plants, which is what makes a wall read as a wall.

Outdoor walls

An exterior wall in Delhi NCR has to survive a 45 degree May and a cold January on the same face. That rules out most of the delicate foliage clients ask for. Hardy species, succulents and tough ground cover types are what actually last, and the palette is more restrained as a result.

Planting density

Walls are planted densely from the start, because a sparse wall never fills in the way clients imagine. It is cheaper to plant it full than to spend a year adding plants into gaps.

Where green walls go

Office receptions and lobbies, restaurant and cafe feature walls, hotel arrival areas, retail interiors, apartment balconies, boundary and compound walls, and facades where a building needs softening. In homes, the most common request is a balcony wall, where a vertical garden gives you planting without losing the floor space.

Maintenance is part of the installation

A living wall needs a visit every two to four weeks to check irrigation, clear blocked emitters, trim growth, feed the plants and replace individual plants that have not taken.

The irrigation is the part that fails quietly. One blocked emitter kills a patch of the wall weeks before it becomes obvious from the floor, and by then the replanting is much larger. This is why we quote the maintenance contract alongside the installation rather than offering it afterwards as an extra.
