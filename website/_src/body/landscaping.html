What a landscaping project involves

Most people picture landscaping as choosing plants. In practice the plants are the last and least risky decision. What determines whether a garden still looks good in three years is what happens under the surface in the weeks before anything is planted.

Site visit and assessment

We look at four things: how the sun tracks across the space through the day, where water goes when it rains hard, what the soil is actually made of, and how the space will be used. That last one changes designs more than anything else. A garden that will host forty people twice a year is a different garden from one where two people drink tea on a Sunday.

Design and planting plan

You get a layout, a planting plan naming every species and quantity, and an itemised quotation. This is the stage to argue, change your mind and ask difficult questions. Everything is cheap to change on paper.

Ground preparation

Levelling, drainage, removal of construction debris, and building up a proper planting medium. On new builds in Delhi NCR this stage is frequently larger than clients expect, because what is described as garden soil turns out to be builder rubble with a covering of earth.

Planting

The visible week. Plants come from our own nursery, which means the design is drawn around stock we know performs in this climate rather than around a catalogue.

Settling in and handover

We return through the first four to six weeks to correct anything struggling, then hand over with a written care plan. A garden that gets the right attention through its first summer will forgive a great deal afterwards.

Where the budget actually goes

On a typical project, ground preparation and hard elements account for more of the cost than the plants do. Paving, edging, drainage, soil replacement, irrigation, lighting and any structure such as a pergola or seating are what move the number.

This is worth knowing when comparing quotations. A conspicuously low quote usually prices the plants properly and leaves the ground work to be discovered later, which is how a project ends up costing more than the higher quote you turned down.

Where to spend, and where to hold back

Two clients with identical budgets can end up with very different gardens depending on where the money lands. This is the split we recommend most often.

Worth spending more on

Soil, drainage and a small number of large specimen plants. Those three do more for how a garden looks and lasts than a longer plant list ever will.

Worth spending less on

Lawn area, decorative features that need constant upkeep, and quantity of small plants. A lawn is the most expensive square footage in a Delhi garden to keep alive through summer.

The kinds of projects we take on

Villas and independent houses

Front lawns, rear gardens, courtyards and driveway planting. Usually one continuous project with the owner closely involved, which suits us.

Housing societies and RWA projects

Common greens, entrance plazas, walking paths, children's play areas and club house surrounds. Society work runs on committee approvals and phased budgets, so we quote the full scheme and can execute it in stages across financial years.

Corporate campuses and offices

Entrance landscaping, courtyards, terrace decks and the planted areas visitors see first. Corporate sites usually need the work done outside business hours, and we plan for that from the start rather than discovering it on day one.

Hotels, resorts and restaurants

Arrival areas, dining courtyards and poolside planting, where the garden is part of what the guest is paying for. These projects tend to be phased around occupancy.

Builders and project sites

Sample flats, marketing suites and handover landscaping, working to a launch date that will not move. Where the timeline is genuinely tight we will tell you what can and cannot establish in that window.

Designing for the Delhi NCR climate

This region asks a lot of a garden. A May afternoon can pass 45 degrees, January nights drop close to freezing, and the monsoon delivers months of rain in a few weeks. A planting scheme that ignores any one of those three fails in that season every year.

Choosing species that survive all three

We build schemes around plants proven in NCR conditions, with tender species used only where they are sheltered and can be looked after. It is a less exotic plant list than some clients arrive expecting, and it is the reason the gardens still look right in year three.

Planning for water

Plants are grouped by water requirement so that irrigation can be zoned sensibly, rather than watering a drought tolerant bed on the same schedule as a lawn. On larger sites this makes a substantial difference to the running cost.

The right season to plant

October to March is the strong window, when roots establish before the heat arrives. Work in other months is possible with shade netting and heavier watering, and it costs a little more in care. We would rather tell you to wait six weeks than plant into a Delhi June and watch the survival rate.

After the garden is handed over

A new garden is at its most vulnerable in its first year. If you have a gardener, we brief them properly on what has been planted and what it needs. If you do not, our garden maintenance contracts pick it up on a monthly or quarterly schedule.
