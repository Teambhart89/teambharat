What happens on a maintenance visit

A visit is not a person with a watering can. Our gardener works through a fixed routine, and the order matters, because half the job is noticing problems while they are still small.

Watering, properly

Watering to depth rather than wetting the surface, and only where the soil actually needs it. More office plants die of overwatering in Delhi NCR than of drought, which surprises almost everyone. If the top two inches are still damp, the plant is skipped that visit.

Pruning, cleaning and shaping

Dead leaves removed, growth shaped, and dust wiped off the foliage. Leaf cleaning sounds cosmetic and is not. A layer of Delhi dust measurably reduces how much light a leaf can use, and indoor plants here have very little light to spare.

Soil and feeding

Soil condition checked, topped up or replaced where it has compacted, and seasonal feeding applied. Feeding is reduced through peak summer, because a plant under heat stress cannot use it and the salts do more harm than the nutrients do good.

Pest and disease checks

Mealybug, spider mite, scale and fungal spotting are the common problems in NCR offices and gardens. Caught in the first week they are a five minute job. Caught in the sixth they are a replacement.

The visit note

Every visit ends with a short written note saying what was done and what needs watching. Clients tell us this is the part they value most, because it turns plant care from something invisible into something they can actually see happening.

Choosing a contract that fits the site

Weekly visits

Suited to offices with a large number of indoor plants, newly installed planting in its first month, and any site where nobody on the premises will touch a plant between visits.

Fortnightly visits

The most common choice for established offices. Frequent enough to catch problems, infrequent enough to keep the cost sensible.

Monthly visits

Works for homes and small gardens that already have someone doing basic watering, where our role is the skilled work rather than the daily routine.

Seasonal adjustment

Schedules are not fixed for the year. Frequency rises through May and June and eases through the cooler months, and outdoor sites move to early morning visits in summer. Watering an exposed garden at midday in a Delhi May does more harm than skipping it.

What a contract covers, and what it does not

Included

  • All scheduled visits at the agreed frequency
  • Watering, pruning, cleaning, feeding and repotting where needed
  • Pest and disease treatment
  • Replacement of plants that fail through normal causes on annual contracts
  • A written note after every visit and a named contact you can call between visits

Charged separately

  • New plants added to expand the planting rather than replace it
  • Losses from causes outside our control, such as construction dust, a burst pipe, or a plant moved into a dark store room
  • Major one time work such as re landscaping a bed or replacing all the soil in a large planter

We would rather set this out plainly at the start than have the conversation later with an invoice in hand.

Taking over plants that are already struggling

About half our maintenance clients come to us with planting that has been neglected for a while, sometimes for years. That is a normal way to start and it does not embarrass anybody.

The audit comes first

The first visit is an audit. You get an honest assessment of which plants are worth saving, which are healthy but in the wrong position and should be moved, and which are past recovering. Then a recovery plan with realistic timelines.

What to expect in the first three months

Slower progress than you want. Recovering neglected planting takes longer than starting fresh, because a stressed plant has to rebuild its root system before it can put out the growth you will actually notice. Month one looks like tidying. Month three starts to look like a garden.
