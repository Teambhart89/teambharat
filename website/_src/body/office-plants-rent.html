Why offices rent plants instead of buying them

Almost every office that buys plants outright ends up in the same place about eight months later. Half the plants look tired, nobody is quite sure whose job the watering was, and someone in admin is getting quotes to replace them. Renting removes that cycle by making the plants somebody else's responsibility, permanently.

What the monthly figure covers

One charge covers the plants, the planters, delivery and placement, a scheduled visit by our gardener, and replacement of anything that stops looking well. There is no separate maintenance invoice and no per plant replacement charge.

How it looks on your books

Renting shifts the spend from a capital purchase to a monthly operating expense, which is often easier to approve than a one time number. It also removes the awkward asset that nobody wants to account for when the office is refitted.

When buying makes more sense

If you have a facility team who genuinely look after the plants, and you expect to stay in the same office for years, buying is cheaper over a long enough horizon. We will supply the plants at wholesale rates and sell you a maintenance contract separately. It is a perfectly reasonable way to do it and we will not push you off it.

Choosing plants for a real office, not a photograph

The constraint in most offices is light, not floor space. A large open floor with glass on one side has a bright edge and a dim middle, and the plants that thrive in the first three metres will slowly fail at fifteen. Matching the species to the position is most of the job.

Plants for cabins and workstations

Snake plant, ZZ plant, money plant and small aglaonema. Compact, tolerant of low light, and undemanding enough to survive a week when the occupant is travelling.

Plants for reception and lobbies

Areca palm, raphis palm, dracaena and large ficus in a good planter. Reception is where a plant does the most work for you, and it is worth spending more per plant and fewer of them.

Plants for meeting rooms and corridors

Peace lily, philodendron and syngonium. Peace lily earns its place in a meeting room by visibly drooping when it is thirsty, which makes it the one plant your team can read without training.

Air purifying plants

Snake plant, areca palm, money plant, peace lily and spider plant are the varieties most often asked for under this heading. They are excellent office plants for many reasons. The air purification effect in an occupied, ventilated office is real but modest, and we would rather say that plainly than sell it as a filtration system.

What we avoid recommending

Flowering plants for permanent indoor positions, anything needing direct sun in an interior cabin, and large specimens in narrow corridors where they will be knocked every day. All three look good in a proposal and disappoint within a season.

How many plants your office needs

A workable starting point is one medium plant for every three to four workstations, plus larger floor plants at reception, in each meeting room, and at the ends of long corridors. Then the plan gets adjusted for the thing that actually decides it, which is where the daylight falls.

Send us a floor plan or a short walkthrough video and you will get a plant count that reflects your office rather than a formula. On most sites the number comes out a little lower than the client expected, and the result looks better for it.

How the rental works

  1. Site visit or photographs

    We look at light, layout and how the space is used. For smaller offices, clear photographs and a floor area are enough to start.

  2. Plant plan and one monthly figure

    You get a proposed plant list by position, a planter selection, and a single monthly rental covering everything.

  3. Delivery and placement

    We deliver and place the plants outside your working hours where possible, so nobody is working around a trolley.

  4. Scheduled care visits

    Weekly or fortnightly, for watering, cleaning, pruning and pest checks, with a short written note left after each visit.

  5. Replacement without a conversation

    If a plant fades, it is swapped on the next visit. You do not need to report it, approve it or pay for it.
