Who we are

We would rather sell you fewer plants that live

Online Plants Cart grew out of nursery supply and moved into services for a simple reason. We kept delivering healthy plants to places that had no plan for keeping them healthy.

How we ended up doing more than selling plants

We started where most nurseries start, growing and supplying plants. The work that followed came from watching what happened to those plants afterwards.

A client would order forty plants for a new office. They would arrive healthy. Six months later we would be back on the same floor, quoting to replace half of them, and the reason was never the plants. It was that nobody had been given the job of watering them, or the areca palm had been placed in a windowless corner because that was where the gap in the furniture was.

So we learned the rest of it. Soil, drainage, irrigation, pest management, and how to design a planting that a real office or a real family can actually keep alive. Today the supply side and the service side are the same team, which is the only arrangement that stops plants falling into the gap between them.

What we believe about plants

The site decides, not the catalogue

Nearly every plant that fails in Delhi NCR failed because it was put somewhere it was never going to survive. Light is the constraint people underestimate most, followed by who is actually going to water it. We ask about both before we quote.

Fewer plants, better placed

Most spaces we visit want more plants than they can support. A smaller number in the right positions, kept healthy, looks better than a full plant list slowly declining. This costs us revenue on the first order and earns it back on the fifth.

Say the inconvenient thing early

If your terrace needs waterproofing before it needs a design, if your reception has no light for a living wall, if the species you have set your heart on will not last a Delhi summer, you should hear it at the quotation stage. It is a shorter conversation then.

Ground preparation is not the boring part

It is the part that determines whether the garden still looks right in year three. On most of our landscaping projects, more of the budget goes below the surface than into the planting, and clients who understand why are the ones who end up happiest.

Who buys plants from us in bulk

Bulk supply is not one kind of customer. A landscaper ordering three hundred shrubs for a site and an HR team ordering forty desk plants want completely different things from us, and the only thing they share is that neither should be paying single unit prices.

Landscapers and garden contractors

You need consistent stock, predictable sizes and a rate you can quote your own client from. Trade buyers get a standing rate list rather than a fresh quotation every week, and we hold regular varieties so a repeat order does not turn into a sourcing exercise.

Corporate offices and workspaces

Reception statements, cabin plants, meeting room greenery and something at the end of every corridor. We suggest species that survive continuous air conditioning and a floor plate with limited daylight, which rules out a surprising amount of what looks good in a catalogue.

Builders, societies and facility teams

Sample flats, club houses, entrance plazas and common greens, usually against a budget that has already been approved and a date that has already been announced. Large orders can be delivered in phases so your site is not storing plants it cannot plant yet.

Hotels, cafes and restaurants

Hardy indoor species that hold up to heavy footfall, constant air conditioning and the occasional spilled drink, chosen to sit with your interior palette rather than argue with it.

Schools, hospitals and institutions

Campus planting, herbal and medicinal gardens for teaching, and air purifying plants for classrooms, wards and waiting areas. Institutional buyers usually need a formal quotation and GST invoice for their procurement process, which we provide as standard.

Retail nurseries and florists

Wholesale stock for resale, in the sizes and quantities that move through a retail counter. If you buy from us regularly, a running account saves a payment cycle on every order.

What you can order at wholesale rates

Everything in our range is available in bulk, and you can mix categories within a single order to reach a quantity slab. There is no requirement to order twenty five of one variety.

Indoor plants

The bulk of office and hospitality orders. These are the varieties that tolerate low light and steady air conditioning without slowly thinning out.

Most requested indoor varieties

Money plant, snake plant, ZZ plant, aglaonema, syngonium, philodendron, peace lily, dracaena, areca palm and raphis palm.

Outdoor and landscaping stock

Ficus, ashoka, bottle palm, bougainvillea, hedge and border plants, ground cover and lawn grass. Sizes run from small liners for mass planting up to established specimens for entrances and feature positions.

A note on specimen sizes

Large specimens are worth ordering early. They are the item most likely to be out of stock at short notice, and growing one on to size is not something we can do inside your project timeline.

Flowering and seasonal plants

Winter and summer seasonals, roses, hibiscus and marigold, used heavily by societies and event teams. Availability follows the growing calendar rather than the order date, so plan seasonal colour a few weeks ahead.

Succulents, cacti and small plants

The volume category for corporate gifting and for anyone furnishing a lot of desks at once. Low maintenance, compact, and forgiving of the watering habits of an average office.

Pots, planters and growing supplies

Ceramic, fibre, FRP, metal and plastic planters, plus potting mix, cocopeat, vermicompost, fertiliser, pebbles and basic tools. Ordering plants and planters together usually saves a delivery and always saves the argument about which pot fits which plant.

Frequently asked questions

What is the minimum quantity for a wholesale plant order?

Wholesale pricing starts at twenty five plants in a single order. You do not need twenty five of the same variety. The slab is worked out on the total quantity, so a mixed order of ten money plants, ten snake plants and five palms qualifies just as well.

Below twenty five plants we are happy to supply you, but at our normal retail rates.

How much lower are wholesale rates compared to retail?

It depends on the variety, the pot and the quantity, but most buyers see somewhere between ten and twenty five percent off the listed retail price, with larger project orders quoted separately. Species that we grow in volume ourselves carry the deepest discount. Imported or slow growing varieties carry the least.

The honest answer is that we quote per order rather than publish one fixed percentage, because pretending otherwise would mean padding the base price first.

How quickly can you deliver a bulk order in Delhi NCR?

Stocked varieties are usually dispatched within 24 to 72 hours of order confirmation and reach anywhere in the NCR the same or next day.

Large project orders of five hundred plants or more, or orders that need a specific size we have to grow on, generally take five to seven working days. Those can be delivered in phases so your site is not holding stock it cannot plant yet.

Do you supply to landscapers, contractors and other nurseries?

Yes, and they make up a good share of what we send out every week. Trade buyers get a standing rate list rather than a fresh quote each time, so you can price your own client work without waiting on us.

If you buy regularly, ask about a running account. It saves a payment cycle on every order.

Can I mix different plants and pot sizes in one order?

Yes. Mixing is normal and it does not cost you the discount. Most office and society orders are a blend of indoor foliage, a few statement plants for reception areas and a run of outdoor stock for the entrance.

The only thing worth grouping is delivery timing. Tender indoor plants and heavy outdoor stock travel better on separate trips if the quantity is large.

Do you provide a GST invoice?

Every order is billed on a proper GST tax invoice, so procurement teams can claim input credit and close their paperwork without chasing us. Purchase order numbers and cost centre references can be printed on the invoice if your finance team needs them.

What happens if plants arrive damaged?

Send us photographs within seven days of delivery and we replace transit damaged plants at no cost. Consignments are packed with root ball protection and staking, so this stays rare, but long summer trips and rough roads do occasionally take a toll.

Damage caused by the site after delivery, such as a plant left in direct afternoon sun for a week without water, is not covered. We will still tell you how to save it.

Do you deliver wholesale plants outside Delhi NCR?

Yes, for orders of around two hundred plants and above we ship pan India by surface transport. Freight is charged at actuals and quoted before you confirm, so there is no surprise at the end.

For smaller quantities outside the NCR the freight usually costs more than the discount saves, and we will tell you that rather than take the order.

Can I visit the nursery and select plants myself?

You are welcome to. Large buyers often want to see the actual stock before committing, especially for feature plants going into a reception or a lobby. Call ahead so we know you are coming and can have the relevant sections ready.

If a visit is not practical, we will send photographs of the exact plants before dispatch.

What are the payment terms on a large order?

Standard bulk orders are confirmed with an advance, with the balance settled before or on delivery. We accept UPI, bank transfer and cheque.

Registered companies buying regularly can request credit terms against a purchase order. That is agreed case by case, usually after the first two or three orders have gone through cleanly.

In short

Corporate plant gifting means supplying live plants in branded pots as gifts for employees, clients or event attendees. Online Plants Cart handles plant selection, logo printing on the planter, gift packing and delivery, from a practical minimum of around fifty units. Popular choices are succulents, snake plant, ZZ plant, lucky bamboo and small aglaonema, all of which survive a desk with little attention.

Why a plant works as a corporate gift

Most corporate gifts have a short life. The hamper is eaten, the diary goes in a drawer, the branded bottle is used twice. A plant sits on the desk, gets watered, gets noticed by the people walking past, and is still there at the next appraisal cycle with your logo on it.

It only works if the plant survives. That is the whole difference between a good plant gift and a slightly awkward one, and it comes down to choosing species that tolerate an air conditioned office and irregular watering.

When companies order

Festive gifting

Diwali is the largest window by a wide margin, followed by New Year. Both are worth planning early, because printing capacity across the market gets booked out and the comfortable timelines belong to whoever ordered in August.

Onboarding and welcome kits

A plant in a branded pot on a new joiner's desk on day one, ideally with a card that names them. Steady volumes through the year rather than a seasonal spike, which makes them easy to schedule.

Client and partner gifts

Smaller quantities, higher value per unit, usually a larger plant in a good ceramic planter with proper boxing.

Events, conferences and CSR

Attendee giveaways, speaker gifts and plantation drive saplings. Volume is high and the per unit budget is usually tight, which suits succulents and small foliage plants well.

Plants that survive a desk

This is a shorter list than clients expect, and keeping to it is why our gift plants are still alive months later.

The reliable choices

What a landscaping project involves

Soil, drainage and a small number of large specimen plants. Those three do more for how a garden looks and lasts than a longer plant list ever will.

What happens on a maintenance visit

A visit is not a person with a watering can. Our gardener works through a fixed routine, and the order matters, because half the job is noticing problems while they are still small.

Watering, properly

Watering to depth rather than wetting the surface, and only where the soil actually needs it. More office plants die of overwatering in Delhi NCR than of drought, which surprises almost everyone. If the top two inches are still damp, the plant is skipped that visit.

Pruning, cleaning and shaping

Dead leaves removed, growth shaped, and dust wiped off the foliage. Leaf cleaning sounds cosmetic and is not. A layer of Delhi dust measurably reduces how much light a leaf can use, and indoor plants here have very little light to spare.

Soil and feeding

Soil condition checked, topped up or replaced where it has compacted, and seasonal feeding applied. Feeding is reduced through peak summer, because a plant under heat stress cannot use it and the salts do more harm than the nutrients do good.

Pest and disease checks

Mealybug, spider mite, scale and fungal spotting are the common problems in NCR offices and gardens. Caught in the first week they are a five minute job. Caught in the sixth they are a replacement.

The visit note

Every visit ends with a short written note saying what was done and what needs watching. Clients tell us this is the part they value most, because it turns plant care from something invisible into something they can actually see happening.

Weekly visits

Suited to offices with a large number of indoor plants, newly installed planting in its first month, and any site where nobody on the premises will touch a plant between visits.

Fortnightly visits

The most common choice for established offices. Frequent enough to catch problems, infrequent enough to keep the cost sensible.

Monthly visits

Vertical gardens and green walls in Delhi NCR

In short

A vertical garden is a wall planted with living plants held in a modular panel or felt pocket system, fed by a drip irrigation line and drained at the base. Online Plants Cart designs, installs and maintains both live and artificial green walls across Delhi NCR. Live walls need irrigation and scheduled upkeep. Artificial walls need neither and suit dark or unreachable positions where a living wall would fail.

How a green wall is actually built

A vertical garden is a system, not a decoration, and understanding the layers explains most of what goes right and wrong with them.

The frame and the gap behind it

A metal or treated batten frame is fixed to the wall so the planting sits a few inches clear of the building surface. That gap is not a detail. It keeps air moving, keeps the wall dry, and is the difference between a green wall and a damp problem.

The waterproof backing

A sealed backing board sits between the planting and the building. Every drop of water that leaves the system is directed downward into a collection channel rather than into the plaster.

The planting layer

Either modular panels holding individual pots, or a felt pocket system where plants root into a fabric medium. Panels are easier to service plant by plant. Felt gives a denser, more continuous surface. We choose between them based on how the wall will be reached for maintenance.

Irrigation and drainage

A drip line runs along the top and feeds down by gravity on a timer, with a channel at the base catching runoff. Many walls run on a recirculating tank, which cuts water use and removes the need for a permanent plumbing connection.

Lighting, where daylight is short

An interior wall with no natural light needs grow lights on a timer. There is no plant selection that avoids this. Where a client would rather not run lighting, we recommend an artificial wall and say so directly.

Live, artificial or moss

These are three different products that happen to look similar in a photograph. Choosing the wrong one is the most common and most expensive mistake in this category.

Live green walls

Right where there is reasonable daylight or a budget for lighting, a water point within reach, and someone accountable for upkeep. A living wall changes through the year, softens sound noticeably in a hard surfaced reception, and reads as genuinely alive in a way nothing else matches.

Artificial green walls

Right for dark corridors, high atrium walls that need scaffolding to reach, exterior positions in harsh western sun, and any site where maintenance will realistically not happen. A good artificial wall kept clean looks far better than a live wall that has been allowed to die, and we would rather install the honest option.

Preserved moss walls

No water, no light, no maintenance beyond keeping them dry and dust free. Excellent in meeting rooms, lift lobbies and interior walls with no services. Keep them out of direct sun and away from humid areas such as an open kitchen, where preserved moss loses its texture over time.

Mixing the three

On large walls we sometimes combine them, using live planting at the reachable lower section and artificial or moss above where access is difficult. Done carefully it reads as one wall and it stays looking right.

Plants that work on a green wall in this climate

Indoor walls

Syngonium, philodendron, money plant, peperomia, ferns and aglaonema. These knit together into a continuous surface rather than sitting as separate plants, which is what makes a wall read as a wall.

Outdoor walls

An exterior wall in Delhi NCR has to survive a 45 degree May and a cold January on the same face. That rules out most of the delicate foliage clients ask for. Hardy species, succulents and tough ground cover types are what actually last, and the palette is more restrained as a result.

Planting density

Walls are planted densely from the start, because a sparse wall never fills in the way clients imagine. It is cheaper to plant it full than to spend a year adding plants into gaps.

Where green walls go

Office receptions and lobbies, restaurant and cafe feature walls, hotel arrival areas, retail interiors, apartment balconies, boundary and compound walls, and facades where a building needs softening. In homes, the most common request is a balcony wall, where a vertical garden gives you planting without losing the floor space.

Maintenance is part of the installation

A living wall needs a visit every two to four weeks to check irrigation, clear blocked emitters, trim growth, feed the plants and replace individual plants that have not taken.

The irrigation is the part that fails quietly. One blocked emitter kills a patch of the wall weeks before it becomes obvious from the floor, and by then the replanting is much larger. This is why we quote the maintenance contract alongside the installation rather than offering it afterwards as an extra.

Frequently asked questions

What is a vertical garden and how does it work?

It is a wall of plants grown in a system fixed to a vertical surface rather than in the ground. Plants sit in modular panels or felt pockets holding a lightweight growing medium. A drip irrigation line runs along the top and feeds down by gravity, and a channel at the base collects the runoff.

The frame is held off the wall on a batten so air moves behind it and the building surface stays dry.

How much does a vertical garden cost per square foot in India?

Live walls carry a much higher cost than artificial ones, and the gap widens once you include the irrigation and the maintenance contract that a live wall genuinely requires.

The variables that move the price most are the system type, plant density, whether grow lights are needed, and how difficult the wall is to reach. We quote per wall after seeing a photograph and the dimensions.

Should I choose a live or an artificial green wall?

Choose live where there is reasonable daylight, a water point nearby, and a budget for ongoing upkeep. It changes with the seasons, softens sound and genuinely feels alive.

Choose artificial for dark corridors, high atrium walls that need scaffolding to reach, exterior positions in harsh sun, and anywhere nobody will take responsibility for maintenance. A well made artificial wall that stays clean looks far better than a live wall that has been left to die.

Which plants work best on a green wall in the Delhi climate?

For indoor walls, syngonium, philodendron, money plant, ferns, peperomia and aglaonema hold up well and knit together into a full surface.

Outdoor walls in Delhi have to survive both a 45 degree May and a cold January, which rules out most of the delicate foliage people ask for. Hardy species and succulents suit outdoor positions far better.

Do vertical gardens damage the wall behind them?

Not when they are built correctly. The frame stands off the wall surface, a waterproof backing board sits between the planting and the building, and all runoff is captured at the base rather than allowed to soak in.

Damage happens on walls where a system has been fixed flat against plaster with no barrier and no drainage. That is a construction failure, not a property of green walls.

How much water does a living wall need?

Less than most people expect, because drip irrigation delivers it precisely rather than flooding. A timer runs short cycles a few times a day, and the schedule shifts by season, more in summer and considerably less in winter.

Many walls can be run on a recirculating tank, which reduces consumption further and removes the need for a permanent plumbing connection.

Can a vertical garden work indoors without natural light?

Only with supplementary lighting. Plants on an interior wall with no daylight will thin out within a few months no matter how well they were chosen.

We install grow lights on a timer where the position justifies it. Where the client would rather not run lights, we recommend an artificial wall and say so plainly.

How long does installation take?

A standard office reception wall is usually installed within two to four days on site once the panels and plants are ready, with a little longer where irrigation needs a new water point or an electrical connection for lighting.

Larger facade projects run to a few weeks and are planned around your site access rather than ours.

Do you make moss walls?

Yes. Preserved moss walls need no water, no light and no maintenance beyond keeping them dry and dust free, which makes them useful in meeting rooms and lift lobbies.

They should be kept out of direct sun and away from humid areas such as an open kitchen or a bathroom wall, where preserved moss loses its texture over time.
